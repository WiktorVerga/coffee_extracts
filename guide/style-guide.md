# Style guide — "Italian coffee, explained" (test page)

> This file is the single source of truth for every post. Claude rereads it on every run.
> Edit it freely: changes apply from the next post on.

---

## 1. What the page is

- **What it is:** an English-language page that explains Italian coffee to people outside Italy, focused on two home methods: the **moka pot** and **home espresso**. Italian bar habits and coffee words come in as context. One idea per carousel, explained well.
- **Promise:** understand the coffee you drink every day, without the snobbery and without the clichés, with simple steps you can follow to make better coffee at home.
- **What it is NOT:** product reviews, sponsorships, news, health advice, or a "you're doing it wrong" page.

## 2. Audience

English speakers, mostly in the US, who make coffee every day and want to make it better at home. Curious, not experts: every post should teach them something new, a fun fact they can try in their own kitchen. They mostly use a moka pot or a home espresso machine, and many are curious about Italy. They read on their phone, quickly: every slide must make sense in 3 seconds.

## 3. Voice

Write in **US English**, second person ("you"). Short sentences, one idea per sentence.

| Trait | Do | Don't |
|---|---|---|
| **Insider, not gatekeeper** | "In Italy, a cappuccino is a morning thing. After lunch, people switch to espresso." | "Real Italians would never…" |
| **Precise** | Concrete numbers and ranges: "25 to 30 seconds" | "A little while" |
| **Curious** | Explain the *why* behind every habit or tip | Lists of rules with no reason |
| **Light** | Dry, gentle humor, at most one joke per carousel | Mocking how other countries drink coffee |
| **Honest** | "It depends on the coffee: taste and adjust." | Absolute promises ("the perfect espresso in 3 steps") |

**Italian words:** keep them in Italian (*moka*, *caffè*, *ristretto*, *macchiato*, *al banco*) and explain each one in plain English the first time it appears in a carousel. Use the correct accents (caffè, not caffe).

**Clichés to avoid:** "Mamma mia", chef's-kiss jokes, mustaches, "like nonna used to make", and any stereotype about Italians.

**Banned words and phrases:** "the secret nobody tells you", "you won't believe", "game-changer", "hack", "ultimate", "mind-blowing", "Here's", "Discover", empty superlatives.

**Emoji:** avoid them. Never on slides; in the caption only in exceptional cases, 1–2 at most, and only if they really add something.

## 4. Fact rules (the most important section)

The system publishes without human review, so:

1. **Never make up** studies, statistics, percentages, quotes, historical dates, or people's names. **Every piece of data must have a source, cited in the post caption** (see section 7). If you can't find a reliable source for a figure or fact, leave it out.
2. Use numbers only when they are **widely accepted** (typical times, temperatures, doses). Prefer **ranges** ("194–205°F") over single values.
3. **Units:** temperatures in °F with °C in parentheses, e.g. "200°F (93°C)". Coffee doses in grams (the standard even in the US). Pressure in bar.
4. **Cultural habits are habits, not laws.** Write "many Italians", "usually", "at most bars", never "Italians always/never".
5. If something is debated or varies a lot, say so: "it depends", "usually", "in general".
6. **No health claims** (caffeine and the body, benefits, risks, daily amounts). If an idea needs one, skip the idea.
7. No brands, products or companies named, positively or negatively.
8. Ideas in the sheet are written **in Italian**. Treat the idea and its notes as the brief: translate the meaning, never translate word for word. Notes and sources in the sheet take priority over Claude's general knowledge.
9. If an idea cannot be covered while following these rules, **don't publish it**: mark it as skipped with the reason.

## 5. Carousel structure

- **Length:** 5 to 10 slides, chosen to fit the idea.
  - simple idea (one concept, one myth, one word) → 5–6 slides
  - how-to or comparison → 7–8 slides
  - rich topic → up to 10, never more
- **Slide 1 — Cover:** promises something specific. Title **8 words max**. It must make people want to swipe.
- **Middle slides:** **one idea per slide**. Use **at least 2 different slide types** (see below) to keep the rhythm.
- **Last slide — Closing:** sums up in one sentence and ends with a simple call to action: save, share, or answer a question.

### Slide types and text limits

| Type | When to use it | Limits |
|---|---|---|
| `cover` | always first | kicker ≤ 4 words, title ≤ 8 words, subtitle ≤ 14 words |
| `text` | explaining a concept | title ≤ 7 words, body ≤ 45 words, highlight (optional) ≤ 12 words |
| `number` | one key figure to remember | number ≤ 6 characters (e.g. "1:2", "200°", "25 s"), caption ≤ 25 words |
| `steps` | procedures, "how to" | title ≤ 7 words, 3 to 5 steps, each ≤ 14 words |
| `compare` | A vs B | title ≤ 6 words, 2 columns, 2–4 points per column, each ≤ 8 words |
| `myth` | checking a common belief | myth ≤ 14 words, verdict `true` or `false`, truth ≤ 35 words |
| `tip` | the practical trick (max 1 per carousel) | body ≤ 30 words |
| `closing` | always last | title ≤ 8 words, body ≤ 20 words, cta ≤ 6 words |

If the text doesn't fit, **shorten the text**, never shrink the type.

## 6. Series

Every idea belongs to one of these series. The sheet's *Rubrica* column is in Italian: use the table to map it. If the column is empty, Claude picks the series.

| Series | Sheet value (Italian) | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| **How-to** | Guida pratica | how to do something (e.g. "Fill a moka pot the right way") |
| **Myth or fact** | Mito o verità | a common belief, checked: state the belief, say right away whether it's true or false, then explain why |
| **Side by side** | Confronto | two things compared (e.g. "Arabica vs robusta"). Turn the data into how it feels in the cup, like a personal experience: "Arabica gives your coffee that slightly tangy note", "Robusta brings the crema everyone loves on an espresso" |
| **Inside the cup** | Dentro la tazza | the physics or chemistry behind it, made simple |
| **One word** | Una parola | one Italian coffee word, explained in a short carousel |
| **Common mistake** | Errore comune | what people often get wrong, and how to fix it |
| **At the bar** | Al bar | how coffee works in an Italian bar: ordering, habits, etiquette |

Avoid publishing the same series twice in a row when the sheet offers alternatives.

## 7. Caption

Fixed structure:

1. **First line:** a hook that is different from the cover title (≤ 125 characters, because that's what shows before "more").
2. **Body:** 2–4 short sentences that **add** something to the slides (context, an example, a bit of culture), without repeating them.
3. **Closing question** for comments, tied to the topic.
4. A blank line, then **Sources:** the source of every figure or fact used in the slides and caption, as "Name of the source (website.com)" separated by semicolons. Links aren't clickable in Instagram captions, so the site name is enough.
5. A blank line, then **3–5 hashtags**: 2 general ones from the base list + 1–3 specific ones.

Total length: about 900 characters max.

**Base hashtags (pick 2):** #coffee #italiancoffee #espresso #mokapot #coffeelover #homebarista

## 8. Final check before publishing

- [ ] The cover promises something specific and the carousel delivers it
- [ ] One idea per slide, no cut-off or tiny text
- [ ] No invented facts, no health claims, no brands, no stereotypes
- [ ] Every figure or fact has its source in the caption's "Sources" line
- [ ] Every Italian word is explained the first time it appears
- [ ] Temperatures in °F with °C in parentheses
- [ ] At least 2 different slide types between cover and closing
- [ ] Caption with hook, body, question, sources and 3–5 hashtags
- [ ] US spelling, none of the banned words
