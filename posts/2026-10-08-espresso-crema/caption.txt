That golden layer on your espresso is mostly gas. Here is what it can and can't tell you.

Crema forms when carbon dioxide from the roast, pushed into the water under pressure, comes back out as tiny bubbles. Coffee oils help hold them in place. Fresher coffee usually has more gas to give.

It is a clue about freshness, not a promise of flavor. Do you judge your shot by the crema, or by the first sip?

Sources: Illy & Navarini, "Neglected Food Bubbles: The Espresso Coffee Foam", Food Biophysics 2011 (link.springer.com)
