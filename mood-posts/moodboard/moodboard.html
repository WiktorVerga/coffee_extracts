@coffee_extracts / mood posts

one cup, one drawing,
one small line

A moodboard for the single-image posts: one cute element that catches the eye and one short line that motivates, feels familiar, or makes the reader look at a small part of their day. Coffee is the thread that ties them together.

01

References

Six images the owner collected. They set the direction. Nothing here is copied, traced or reused: we take the ideas listed under each one and draw and write our own.

The refs/ folder is local only and not committed. If the images are missing, the notes above still describe what each one contributes.

02

What makes them work

Seven things the six references have in common. Every mood post should pass all of them.

One ideaOne line or one drawing gag. The reader gets it in under two seconds, without a second sentence explaining the first.
Mostly emptyText and art together cover a third of the canvas at most. The empty space is what makes it look calm and "aesthetic".
Two or three colorsA background, an ink, and at most one accent. All of them come from the coffee range: cream, sand, caramel, brown, near-black.
One small drawingA single object or character, never a scene. It is either tiny next to the text or the star of the post. Never in between.
Type has a personalityLowercase serif reads warm and intimate. Condensed caps read deadpan. The same line changes meaning with the typeface, so pick on purpose.
One word carries itMost lines have a word that lands: a switch to italic, to caps, or to the accent color. One word, not three.
Flat and hand-madeNo gradients, shadows, photos or textures. Slightly imperfect lines. It should look drawn by a person in ten minutes.

04

Typography

Four font keys and three ways to mark the key word. The theme decides both, so the writer only marks the word with *asterisks*.

serif

slow morning
Fraunces, crisp (SOFT 0), high optical size. Weights 300 to 500. Used by espresso, latte, paper. Echoes ref 3 (cream serif on dark) and ref 5 (light serif on beige).

serif-soft

second cup
Fraunces with SOFT 100 and WONK 1: round, friendly, fat at 900. Used by mocha (900) and caramel (600). Echoes the fat serif of ref 4.

narrow

not yet
Archivo Narrow 700, uppercase, slightly open tracking. Used by oat. Echoes the deadpan caps of ref 6, with a cleaner face in place of the hand lettering.

display

refill
Archivo Black. No theme uses it today: it is kept in reserve for a future loud theme or a one-word post. Closest echo is the heavy last word of ref 5.

slow mornings are still mornings

italicFraunces Italic in the accent color. Soft, like lowering your voice. Espresso and caramel. Our own addition to ref 3.

small pot, big plans

accentSame face, accent color only. A change of tone, not of volume. Mocha, oat and paper. Echoes the two-tone look of ref 4.

rest is also on the menu

bold-capsUppercase, weight 900, accent color. The loudest switch, so it takes one word only. Latte. Echoes ref 5.

Cute elements

The drawing library. Seven motifs are sketched here to fix the line style. All are original drawings, colored only through the three art variables, so each one works on every theme.

Objects from a kitchen counter or a cafe table. No people, no hands, no logos, no cup that looks like a known chain's cup.

Drawing rules

  • Line: one weight, 6 to 9 units on a 400 box, round caps and joins, slightly wobbly curves. Small details may drop to the thin end.
  • Fill: one coffee-colored fill per drawing (the coffee, a sleeve, a handle). Bodies and cups use the paper color. Blush is the same fill at half opacity.
  • Flat: no gradients, no shadows, no texture, no background shape.
  • Readable small: the drawing must still read at 250 px wide. If a detail disappears, remove it.

What makes it cute

  • Big head, tiny limbs. The body is mostly one round shape. Arms are stubs, legs are optional.
  • Dot eyes, set low and wide apart. Closed eyes are two small arcs. The mouth is tiny or missing.
  • One feeling per drawing: sleepy, content, or mildly overwhelmed. Never angry, never winking.
  • One small extra: a curl of steam, a "z", a heart. Stop there.
06

Layouts

Five compositions, all 4:5. The circle is the drawing, the bars are the line, the short dark bar is the emphasized word.

center

The default. Art on top, text centered under it. Use for 5 to 12 words when the drawing and the line matter equally.

stack

Big left-aligned text, one or two words per line, small art tucked beside it. Use for 3 to 7 punchy words with natural line breaks.

hero

The drawing is the post, about 60% of the canvas, with a short caption under it. Use when the line only works with the character.

side

Art on the left, text on the right, centered together. Use for two or three short lines next to a tall drawing such as the moka or a paper cup.

quiet

Small text in a lot of empty space, no art or a tiny one above. Use for reflective lines that should feel like a whisper. At most one in every four posts.

08

Voice of the lines

The line sounds like a friend talking across a small table: warm, a little dry, never selling anything. It is about the person and their day. Coffee is the excuse.

Three kinds of line

  • Motivational. Permission to start small or start again. The reader should feel lighter, not pushed. "the first cup doesn't have to be perfect. neither do you."
  • Relatable. A small true thing people recognize and send to a friend. The reader should think "that's me". "made coffee. forgot coffee. reheated coffee."
  • Small reflection. One moment of the day, looked at closely. The reader should pause for a second. "who taught you how to make your coffee?"

Do

  • Keep it between 1 and 14 words. Six is a good number.
  • Write in lowercase, first person or "you", present tense.
  • Use concrete things: the cup, the moka, the kitchen, the first sip, the walk to work.
  • Let one word carry the line and mark only that one.
  • Be kind. The joke is on the morning, never on the reader.
  • Leave something unsaid. The caption can add one thought.

Don't

  • No hustle talk: grind, rise and shine, crush the day, no days off.
  • No health or energy claims, no caffeine doses, no "coffee makes you...".
  • No well-known quotes or slogans, and none of the six reference lines.
  • No "addicted", no "can't function", no jokes at other people's expense.
  • No emoji, hashtags, brand names or exclamation marks in the image.
  • No puns that need explaining, and no second sentence that explains the first.
